# I started reading again.

I am a 90s' kid and I remember the excitement of waiting for a new edition of magazines and comic books. It was a different world, a slower world, where we had to wait for things and that waiting made them more special. I vividly remember the smell of old books, the crispness of the new ones, the satisfaction of turning each page and the anticipation of what is about to come on the next pages. It was a tangible and sensory experience that is hard to replicate in the digital age. Now screens are omnipresent and I am writing on one.

Time passed, I grew up through the times when first keypad phones came and then screens took over. Gradually these screens replaced everything. One by one, my books were replaced by blogs, articles, social media, youtube and then short videos. I consumed more information but I rarely retained any of it. 2013 was the year when high school ended, and I moved to Kota to prepare for JEE (an entrance exam for engineering colleges in India). I left home, with that I left my world of fiction books and replaced it with textbooks and notebooks. 

Even in high school, I had textbooks but it coexisted with my story books, in Kota, books meant only the textbooks for JEE. After 2 years, I left for college and then my interaction with Digital world killed my liking for the print world. I would always try to look for PDF version of textbooks which could easily work with my laptop and slowly reading was limited just to learn the coursework.

Fast forward to 2020, COVID-19 pandemic hit us all and forced us to stay inside. When many of us were rediscovering hobbies, I was stuck in Noida and with some books that the previous tenants in my apartment had left behind. I picked up Catcher in the Rye, and I was hooked. After that I moved back to home spent some time and forgot reading again. 

Cut to 2021, I moved to Bengaluru and started a new job. My life was mostly work, eat and sleep. I decided to gift myself two things, a new bicycle and a mystery box of books.

I got a set of 15 books which were mostly novels from Khaled Hosseini, Audrey Niffenegger, Dan Brown and a few others. I started reading these books, but it took me months to finish them. But slowly, I started craving that feeling of true immersion, of getting lost in someone else's world or grappling with a new idea that only a book can provide. I don't think I have still finished all those mystry books though. But that set out something, I started looking for more books to read, I thought fictions were waste of time, so I started reading more non-fictions and biographies. Eventually these all non-fiction started feeling like textbooks and I again stopped reading.

I started reading properly in 2023 again. I started logging my reads on [Goodreads](https://www.goodreads.com/user/show/129681320-akhilesh-kumar) and took an year challenge to read atleast 6 books.

![Sinking into a good book after years of digital noise](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/akhilesh-k/blog-content/refs/heads/main/markdown/i-started-reading-again/IMG_2668.JPG)


The first few books were a bit of a struggle, I would be reading a page and suddenly I would find myself scrolling my phone. It took me months to finish the first book. And honestly somewhere I finished that book for my Goodreads streak rather than for myself. I think I eneded up reading around 12-14 books. This was the time, I met Anurag at my work he happened to be my lead engineer and eventually manager. When he joined our team that worked on Search bar for the ecommerce company, he finished Lucene in action within first few weeks of starting. Why this is worth mentioning? I think this was the time, I started understanding that reading books is not just for getting marks in exams but to understand something deeply. Since I also started reading consistently, I really felt what it means to deeply understand a subject. Our exchanges started revolving around book recommendations which used to be very fun and insightful.

Moving on to 2025, I gifted myself a Kindle on my birthday. I was a bit hesitant initially thinking it would be just another gadget that I would stop using after few weeks. But that was not the case. I started reading more than ever. The best thing about Kindle is I carry it everywhere. I have carried it to mountains, beaches, grocery shopping, work commute, heck, even dates too!

![Kindle and me, probably the best investment I have ever made](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/akhilesh-k/blog-content/refs/heads/main/markdown/i-started-reading-again/kindle_and_me.png)

I’m reading during my commute, before bed instead of staring at a screen, and even sneaking in a few pages during lunch. There's no pressure to finish a certain number of books, or to stick to a particular book, I keep reading 2-3 at a time. It’s simply about the pleasure of reading itself.

![taking kindle on dates!](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/akhilesh-k/blog-content/refs/heads/main/markdown/i-started-reading-again/taking_kindles_on_date.jpg)

If you, like me, have found yourself drifting away from reading, I can’t recommend enough just picking up that one book. The one that’s been calling to you, however quietly. You might be surprised at what you rediscover, not just within its pages, but within yourself too.

Right now, I'm halfway through "Project Hail Mary" by Andy Weir, and absolutely loving it. What's on your nightstand, or what book are you planning to dive into next?
