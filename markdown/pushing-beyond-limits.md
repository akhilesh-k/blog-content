# Pushing Beyond Limits

For the longest time, I believed progress came from motivation. If I felt excited, I trained. If I did not, I postponed. That worked for a while, until it did not.

Early this year I realized I was waiting for perfect days that never came. Work was busy, sleep was inconsistent, and every week had a reason to skip one more run. The breakthrough was not a fancy plan. It was simple: I promised myself I would show up, even if the session was average.

Some mornings I ran strong, some mornings I shuffled through five tired kilometers, but those small days mattered more than the heroic days. Consistency started changing everything. My legs felt stronger, recovery became easier, and my mind stopped panicking at discomfort.

I also learned the injury lesson the hard way. Skipping warm-up and proper stretching always looked like a "small" shortcut until pain forced me to stop. Since then, pre-run mobility and post-run stretching are non-negotiable.

The biggest lesson was mental, not physical. I learned that "limits" are often just stories we repeat when things get hard. When I kept moving anyway, those stories started losing power.

I am still far from where I want to be, but I am no longer the person who waits for motivation. I train because I said I would. And that has been the most liberating shift of all.
