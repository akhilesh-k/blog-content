# Training for a Triathlon

When I first said out loud that I wanted to do a triathlon, most people asked the obvious question: "Do you even swim regularly?" The honest answer was no.

Running felt familiar. Cycling felt manageable. Swimming felt like starting from zero. I am still in the learning phase for swimming, and that has been the humbling part of this journey.

What helped me build confidence was the bike. I have done many 100K rides around Bangalore, and those long sessions gave me the endurance base to think seriously about triathlon training.

I began with a simple rhythm instead of an aggressive plan: three runs, two rides, and two swim sessions every week. Nothing heroic. Just repeatable. Some weeks were smooth, some were messy, but the routine gave me structure.

The hardest lesson was pacing. Early on, I attacked every session like race day and ended up exhausted by Thursday. Later, I learned to respect easy days. Once I slowed down in training, I actually got faster where it mattered.

Brick sessions were a different kind of challenge. Getting off the bike and trying to run with heavy legs felt awkward and frustrating. But after a few weeks, the body adapted. The transition stopped feeling like panic and started feeling planned.

Triathlon taught me that endurance is less about suffering and more about discipline. Train with patience, recover with intent, and trust that small sessions stack into real confidence.
