# Marking the 100k ride off my bucketlist

A 100 km ride sat on my bucket list for a long time because it felt just far enough to be intimidating.

The route was Bangalore -> Hosur -> Krishnagiri (Tamil Nadu). Having a clear destination helped me stay mentally engaged through the long hours.

I had done plenty of medium rides, but this one carried a different mental weight. Triple digits. Long hours. No shortcuts once you are deep into the route.

The first 40 km were smooth and almost relaxing. Then the heat picked up, headwinds appeared, and hydration started to matter more than pace. That is where long rides stop being about excitement and become about discipline.

I focused on small checkpoints: fuel every 30-40 minutes, sip regularly, keep cadence steady, and avoid sudden efforts on climbs. Nothing fancy, just execution.

By the final stretch, I was tired but calm. When my bike computer rolled past 100.0, I felt equal parts relief and pride. Not because of speed, but because I stayed patient for the full distance.

This ride reminded me that endurance goals are rarely won by talent alone. They are won by planning, pacing, and refusing to panic when things get uncomfortable.
