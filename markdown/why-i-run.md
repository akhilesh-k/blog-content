# Why I run?

People usually assume I run for fitness. That is true, but it is only part of the story.

Most of this journey has happened in Bangalore. The city traffic is chaotic, but early mornings here can be unexpectedly peaceful, and those quiet runs have become my anchor.

I still remember my first 5K timing: 36 minutes. Over time, with consistent training, I brought it down to around 24 minutes. That shift did not come from one breakthrough workout. It came from months of ordinary days done well.

Right now I am taking it slow again after my fibula fracture surgery in April 2025. Recovery has changed my relationship with running. I am less obsessed with pace and more focused on staying healthy enough to keep showing up.

I run because it keeps me honest. If I skip too often, the body tells me. If I stay regular, the rewards show up quietly: better energy, better mood, better focus.

Running has become my reset button. It does not solve everything, but it makes everything feel more manageable.
